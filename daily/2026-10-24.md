# Daily English — 2026-10-24

Focus: intermediate vocabulary for daily personal life + working professionals.

## Words (10)

### 1. allocate
- **Meaning:** To set aside time, money, or resources for a specific purpose.
- **Usage:** We need to allocate two engineers to the billing fix this week.

### 2. calibrate
- **Meaning:** To adjust something so it matches a standard or expected level.
- **Usage:** Let’s calibrate our estimates after last sprint’s surprises.

### 3. dwindle
- **Meaning:** To become gradually smaller or fewer.
- **Usage:** Interest in the old app will dwindle once the new version launches.

### 4. facilitate
- **Meaning:** To make a process or discussion easier to happen.
- **Usage:** Can you facilitate the workshop while I take notes?

### 5. genuine
- **Meaning:** Real and sincere, not fake or forced.
- **Usage:** Her apology felt genuine, so the team moved on quickly.

### 6. impede
- **Meaning:** To slow down or block progress.
- **Usage:** Frequent context-switching will impede deep work on the migration.

### 7. navigate
- **Meaning:** To find your way through a situation, system, or place.
- **Usage:** New joiners need help to navigate the internal tools and approvals.

### 8. omit
- **Meaning:** To leave something out, either by choice or by mistake.
- **Usage:** Don’t omit the rollback steps from the release checklist.

### 9. resilient
- **Meaning:** Able to recover quickly from problems or stress.
- **Usage:** Building a resilient on-call culture matters more than blaming individuals.

### 10. uphold
- **Meaning:** To support or maintain a rule, standard, or promise.
- **Usage:** We uphold customer privacy even when a shortcut would be faster.

## Phrases (10)

### 1. bite the bullet
- **Meaning:** Do something difficult or unpleasant because it must be done.
- **Usage:** We should bite the bullet and rewrite the flaky test suite.

### 2. draw the line
- **Meaning:** Set a clear limit on what you will accept.
- **Usage:** I draw the line at weekend meetings unless there’s a production outage.

### 3. get the ball rolling
- **Meaning:** Start an activity or process.
- **Usage:** Let’s get the ball rolling with a short kickoff tomorrow morning.

### 4. hit the ground running
- **Meaning:** Start a new job or project with energy and little delay.
- **Usage:** She hit the ground running in her first week and shipped a small fix.

### 5. keep something under wraps
- **Meaning:** Keep something secret for now.
- **Usage:** Keep the launch date under wraps until marketing is ready.

### 6. lose track of time
- **Meaning:** Become so focused that you forget how late it is.
- **Usage:** I lost track of time debugging and missed dinner with friends.

### 7. nip it in the bud
- **Meaning:** Stop a small problem before it grows bigger.
- **Usage:** Let’s nip the rumor in the bud before it spreads across the team.

### 8. pull your weight
- **Meaning:** Do your fair share of the work.
- **Usage:** Everyone needs to pull their weight during the release week.

### 9. read between the lines
- **Meaning:** Understand a hidden meaning that isn’t said directly.
- **Usage:** If you read between the lines, the client is worried about timelines.

### 10. take a rain check
- **Meaning:** Politely decline now but suggest doing it another time.
- **Usage:** I’ll take a rain check on coffee today — can we do Thursday instead?
