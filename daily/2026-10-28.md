# Daily English — 2026-10-28

Focus: intermediate vocabulary for daily personal life + working professionals.

## Words (10)

### 1. avert
- **Meaning:** To prevent something bad from happening.
- **Usage:** Early alerts helped us avert a full outage during peak traffic.

### 2. coalesce
- **Meaning:** To come together and form one clear whole.
- **Usage:** After the workshop, three rough ideas coalesced into a single roadmap.

### 3. contend
- **Meaning:** To argue a point; to deal with a difficult situation.
- **Usage:** I contend that we should ship the MVP before adding more options.

### 4. discern
- **Meaning:** To notice or understand something that is not obvious.
- **Usage:** It took a few demos to discern what users actually struggled with.

### 5. forgo
- **Meaning:** To decide not to have or do something you want.
- **Usage:** I’ll forgo the team dinner tonight so I can finish the review.

### 6. juxtapose
- **Meaning:** To place two things side by side to compare them.
- **Usage:** The slide juxtaposes last month’s churn with this month’s retention gains.

### 7. pertain
- **Meaning:** To relate or apply to something.
- **Usage:** Please only comment on points that pertain to the billing module.

### 8. reconcile
- **Meaning:** To make two conflicting things agree; to restore a good relationship.
- **Usage:** We need to reconcile the finance report with the payment gateway totals.

### 9. stipulate
- **Meaning:** To state a condition clearly as part of an agreement.
- **Usage:** The contract stipulates a 48-hour response time for critical incidents.

### 10. withstand
- **Meaning:** To remain strong despite pressure, stress, or difficulty.
- **Usage:** The new API design should withstand a sudden spike in traffic.

## Phrases (10)

### 1. a tough nut to crack
- **Meaning:** A problem or person that is hard to deal with.
- **Usage:** The flaky integration tests are a tough nut to crack.

### 2. bring something to the table
- **Meaning:** Offer a useful skill, idea, or contribution.
- **Usage:** She brings strong customer empathy to the table in every design review.

### 3. cut someone some slack
- **Meaning:** Be less critical of someone; give them more patience.
- **Usage:** Cut him some slack — he just joined and is still learning the tools.

### 4. get wind of
- **Meaning:** Hear about something, often unofficially.
- **Usage:** I got wind of a possible reorg, but nothing is confirmed yet.

### 5. give someone the benefit of the doubt
- **Meaning:** Assume someone is honest or correct until proven otherwise.
- **Usage:** Let’s give the vendor the benefit of the doubt until we see the logs.

### 6. hit a snag
- **Meaning:** Run into an unexpected problem.
- **Usage:** We hit a snag with the SSL certificate just before go-live.

### 7. keep something at bay
- **Meaning:** Prevent something unwanted from getting close or worse.
- **Usage:** Regular backups help keep data-loss risk at bay.

### 8. put your finger on something
- **Meaning:** Identify exactly what is wrong or important.
- **Usage:** I can’t put my finger on why the page feels slow, but something’s off.

### 9. smooth things over
- **Meaning:** Reduce tension after a disagreement or mistake.
- **Usage:** A quick apology call helped smooth things over with the client.

### 10. throw in the towel
- **Meaning:** Give up after finding something too difficult.
- **Usage:** Don’t throw in the towel after one failed experiment — try one more iteration.
