# Daily English — 2026-10-23

Focus: intermediate vocabulary for daily personal life + working professionals.

## Words (10)

### 1. assess
- **Meaning:** To carefully judge the value, quality, or impact of something.
- **Usage:** Let’s assess the risk before we roll out the change to production.

### 2. collaborate
- **Meaning:** To work together with others on a shared task.
- **Usage:** Design and engineering need to collaborate more closely on this feature.

### 3. defer
- **Meaning:** To delay something until a later time; to respect someone else’s decision.
- **Usage:** We’ll defer the redesign discussion until next sprint.

### 4. endorse
- **Meaning:** To publicly support or approve something.
- **Usage:** The manager endorsed the proposal in today’s leadership meeting.

### 5. gauge
- **Meaning:** To measure or estimate a situation, feeling, or reaction.
- **Usage:** I sent a quick poll to gauge interest in a Friday team lunch.

### 6. hinder
- **Meaning:** To make it harder for something to happen or progress.
- **Usage:** Unclear requirements will hinder our ability to deliver on time.

### 7. justify
- **Meaning:** To give a valid reason for a decision or action.
- **Usage:** Can you justify the extra budget for this tool?

### 8. persuade
- **Meaning:** To convince someone to do or believe something.
- **Usage:** She persuaded her parents to try the new restaurant this weekend.

### 9. refrain
- **Meaning:** To stop yourself from doing something.
- **Usage:** Please refrain from sharing screenshots of customer data on Slack.

### 10. scrutinize
- **Meaning:** To examine something very carefully.
- **Usage:** Finance will scrutinize every line item before approving the invoice.

## Phrases (10)

### 1. back to square one
- **Meaning:** Having to start over after a plan fails.
- **Usage:** The API contract changed again, so we’re back to square one on integration.

### 2. bring someone up to speed
- **Meaning:** Quickly update someone on what they missed.
- **Usage:** Can you bring Ankit up to speed before the client call?

### 3. come up with
- **Meaning:** To think of an idea, plan, or solution.
- **Usage:** We need to come up with a backup plan if the vendor is delayed.

### 4. get cold feet
- **Meaning:** Suddenly feel nervous and want to back out of a decision.
- **Usage:** He got cold feet about switching jobs at the last minute.

### 5. in light of
- **Meaning:** Considering new information or a recent situation.
- **Usage:** In light of the outage, we’re delaying the marketing campaign.

### 6. leave no stone unturned
- **Meaning:** Try every possible way to find a solution.
- **Usage:** The team left no stone unturned while debugging the payment failure.

### 7. in the same boat
- **Meaning:** In the same difficult situation as someone else.
- **Usage:** Don’t worry — we’re all in the same boat with this tight deadline.

### 8. put your best foot forward
- **Meaning:** Try your hardest to make a good impression.
- **Usage:** Put your best foot forward in tomorrow’s interview.

### 9. see eye to eye
- **Meaning:** Fully agree with someone.
- **Usage:** We don’t see eye to eye on the design, but we can still ship a middle option.

### 10. think on your feet
- **Meaning:** Respond quickly and smartly without preparation.
- **Usage:** Live demos force you to think on your feet when something breaks.
