# Daily English — 2026-10-20

Focus: intermediate vocabulary for daily personal life + working professionals.

## Words (10)

### 1. mitigate
- **Meaning:** To make a problem less severe or harmful.
- **Usage:** We added extra testing to mitigate the risk of another production issue.

### 2. nuanced
- **Meaning:** Having subtle differences; not simple black-and-white.
- **Usage:** Her feedback was nuanced — she liked the idea but wanted clearer messaging.

### 3. forthcoming
- **Meaning:** Willing to share information; also means “coming soon.”
- **Usage:** He wasn’t very forthcoming about why the delivery was delayed.

### 4. bottleneck
- **Meaning:** A point that slows down the whole process.
- **Usage:** Waiting for approvals has become the biggest bottleneck in this project.

### 5. consolidate
- **Meaning:** To combine several things into one stronger or clearer whole.
- **Usage:** Let’s consolidate all feedback into one document before the review.

### 6. hesitant
- **Meaning:** Unsure or slow to decide because of doubt.
- **Usage:** I’m hesitant to book the tickets until we confirm the holiday dates.

### 7. thorough
- **Meaning:** Complete and careful; covering every important detail.
- **Usage:** Please do a thorough check of the invoice before we pay it.

### 8. reassure
- **Meaning:** To say or do something that makes someone feel less worried.
- **Usage:** A short message from the doctor reassured her that everything looked fine.

### 9. overlook
- **Meaning:** To miss or fail to notice something.
- **Usage:** Don’t overlook the small print in the contract — it affects renewal terms.

### 10. streamline
- **Meaning:** To make a process simpler and more efficient.
- **Usage:** We’re trying to streamline onboarding so new joiners settle faster.

## Phrases (10)

### 1. give someone a heads-up
- **Meaning:** Warn or inform someone in advance.
- **Usage:** Just giving you a heads-up — I might join the call five minutes late.

### 2. close the loop
- **Meaning:** Finish a discussion by confirming the outcome with everyone involved.
- **Usage:** I’ll close the loop with the vendor once we receive the revised quote.

### 3. get up to speed
- **Meaning:** Learn enough to understand the current situation.
- **Usage:** Can you help me get up to speed on what changed while I was on leave?

### 4. take this offline
- **Meaning:** Continue a discussion privately, not in the larger meeting/group.
- **Usage:** That’s a good point — let’s take this offline after the call.

### 5. set expectations
- **Meaning:** Clearly say what will happen and by when.
- **Usage:** Let’s set expectations early: the first draft will be ready by Thursday.

### 6. on short notice
- **Meaning:** With very little time to prepare.
- **Usage:** Thanks for joining on short notice — I know this meeting was sudden.

### 7. go the extra mile
- **Meaning:** Do more than what is normally expected.
- **Usage:** She went the extra mile and prepared sample answers for the client demo.

### 8. clear the air
- **Meaning:** Resolve tension by talking openly about a problem.
- **Usage:** We need to clear the air about yesterday’s misunderstanding.

### 9. put something on hold
- **Meaning:** Temporarily pause something.
- **Usage:** Let’s put the redesign on hold until the budget is approved.

### 10. for what it’s worth
- **Meaning:** Used when offering an opinion that may or may not help.
- **Usage:** For what it’s worth, I think the shorter version sounds more natural.
