# Daily English — 2026-10-26

Focus: intermediate vocabulary for daily personal life + working professionals.

## Words (10)

### 1. articulate
- **Meaning:** To express an idea clearly in words.
- **Usage:** She articulated the customer problem better than the written brief.

### 2. bypass
- **Meaning:** To avoid a usual process or path, often to save time.
- **Usage:** Don’t bypass the approval step just to push a hotfix.

### 3. candid
- **Meaning:** Honest and direct, even when the truth is uncomfortable.
- **Usage:** I need your candid feedback on whether this design is usable.

### 4. distill
- **Meaning:** To reduce something complex to its most important points.
- **Usage:** Can you distill the research into three key recommendations?

### 5. embed
- **Meaning:** To fix something firmly inside a larger system or habit.
- **Usage:** We want to embed accessibility checks into every PR review.

### 6. frustrate
- **Meaning:** To make someone feel annoyed because progress is blocked.
- **Usage:** Unclear ownership will frustrate everyone working on the launch.

### 7. iterate
- **Meaning:** To improve something through repeated small changes.
- **Usage:** Let’s iterate on the onboarding flow after the first user tests.

### 8. leverage
- **Meaning:** To use something you already have to get a better result.
- **Usage:** We can leverage last quarter’s survey data instead of starting over.

### 9. safeguard
- **Meaning:** To protect something from harm or risk.
- **Usage:** Extra logging will help safeguard customer data during the cutover.

### 10. underscore
- **Meaning:** To emphasize or stress the importance of something.
- **Usage:** The outage underscores why we need better on-call runbooks.

## Phrases (10)

### 1. call the shots
- **Meaning:** Be the person who makes the important decisions.
- **Usage:** Product will call the shots on which feature ships first.

### 2. dig deeper
- **Meaning:** Investigate more carefully beyond the surface.
- **Usage:** The metrics look fine, but we should dig deeper into the user drop-off.

### 3. get your foot in the door
- **Meaning:** Get a first opportunity that may lead to more.
- **Usage:** The freelance project helped him get his foot in the door at the company.

### 4. get the green light
- **Meaning:** Receive official approval to start or continue.
- **Usage:** We can’t hire contractors until we get the green light from finance.

### 5. lay the groundwork
- **Meaning:** Do early work that makes later success possible.
- **Usage:** This spike will lay the groundwork for the full rewrite next quarter.

### 6. miss the boat
- **Meaning:** Lose an opportunity by acting too late.
- **Usage:** If we delay the campaign, we might miss the boat on the festival season.

### 7. raise the bar
- **Meaning:** Set a higher standard than before.
- **Usage:** The new design system raises the bar for consistency across apps.

### 8. sit tight
- **Meaning:** Wait patiently without taking action yet.
- **Usage:** Sit tight while we check with legal before responding to the client.

### 9. take the lead
- **Meaning:** Be the main person responsible for driving something.
- **Usage:** Can you take the lead on coordinating the release checklist?

### 10. zero in on
- **Meaning:** Focus closely on one specific thing.
- **Usage:** Let’s zero in on the payment failure path for this week’s fix.
