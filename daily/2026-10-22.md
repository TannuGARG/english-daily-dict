# Daily English — 2026-10-22

Focus: intermediate vocabulary for daily personal life + working professionals.

## Words (10)

### 1. acknowledge
- **Meaning:** To accept or admit that something is true; to show you received a message.
- **Usage:** Please acknowledge this email so I know you received the invite.

### 2. negotiate
- **Meaning:** To discuss something to reach an agreement.
- **Usage:** We need to negotiate a better timeline with the vendor.

### 3. convey
- **Meaning:** To communicate a message, feeling, or information clearly.
- **Usage:** Please convey my thanks to the team for staying late on the release.

### 4. anticipate
- **Meaning:** To expect something and prepare for it.
- **Usage:** We anticipate higher traffic this weekend, so let’s scale the servers.

### 5. resolve
- **Meaning:** To find a solution to a problem; to decide firmly.
- **Usage:** The support team resolved the payment issue within an hour.

### 6. contribute
- **Meaning:** To give ideas, effort, or help toward a shared goal.
- **Usage:** Everyone can contribute one suggestion for the weekend plan.

### 7. discourage
- **Meaning:** To make someone less confident or less willing to do something.
- **Usage:** A few failed attempts shouldn’t discourage you from practicing English daily.

### 8. outline
- **Meaning:** To give the main points of a plan without full detail.
- **Usage:** Can you outline the migration steps before tomorrow’s review?

### 9. compromise
- **Meaning:** An agreement where each side gives up something; to settle by meeting in the middle.
- **Usage:** Let’s compromise — dinner at 8 instead of 7, and a shorter movie.

### 10. emphasize
- **Meaning:** To give special importance to something.
- **Usage:** In the mail, emphasize that the payment deadline is Friday.

## Phrases (10)

### 1. at the end of the day
- **Meaning:** When everything is considered; ultimately.
- **Usage:** At the end of the day, customer trust matters more than shipping early.

### 2. by and large
- **Meaning:** In general; mostly.
- **Usage:** By and large, the new onboarding flow has been smooth.

### 3. get the hang of
- **Meaning:** To learn how to do something after some practice.
- **Usage:** It took a week, but I’ve finally gotten the hang of the new dashboard.

### 4. keep someone posted
- **Meaning:** Continue sending updates to someone.
- **Usage:** Keep me posted if the courier is delayed.

### 5. make sense of
- **Meaning:** To understand something that seems confusing.
- **Usage:** I’m still trying to make sense of these billing numbers.

### 6. on the fence
- **Meaning:** Unable to decide between two options.
- **Usage:** I’m on the fence about renewing the gym membership this month.

### 7. play it by ear
- **Meaning:** Decide as you go, without a fixed plan.
- **Usage:** We don’t have a fixed itinerary — we’ll play it by ear in Goa.

### 8. run by someone
- **Meaning:** Share an idea with someone to get their opinion or approval.
- **Usage:** Let me run this draft by Legal before we publish it.

### 9. take something into account
- **Meaning:** Consider something when making a decision.
- **Usage:** Please take traffic into account when estimating the arrival time.

### 10. under pressure
- **Meaning:** Feeling stressed because of deadlines or expectations.
- **Usage:** She stays calm even when the team is under pressure before a launch.
