# Daily English — 2026-11-18

Focus: intermediate vocabulary for daily personal life + working professionals.

## Words (10)

### 1. bolster
- **Meaning:** To support or strengthen something.
- **Usage:** Extra test coverage will bolster our confidence before the release.

### 2. curtail
- **Meaning:** To reduce or limit something, often suddenly.
- **Usage:** We had to curtail travel spending for the rest of the quarter.

### 3. ensue
- **Meaning:** To happen afterward, often as a result.
- **Usage:** After the outage announcement, a long discussion ensued in Slack.

### 4. evoke
- **Meaning:** To bring a feeling, memory, or image into the mind.
- **Usage:** The welcome kit was designed to evoke a sense of belonging for new joiners.

### 5. fortify
- **Meaning:** To make something stronger against attack or failure.
- **Usage:** We fortified the login flow with rate limiting after the spike in failed attempts.

### 6. glean
- **Meaning:** To collect information bit by bit from what is available.
- **Usage:** From the support tickets, we gleaned that users struggle most with password reset.

### 7. heed
- **Meaning:** To pay careful attention to advice or a warning.
- **Usage:** Heed the rollback plan — shipping without one is risky.

### 8. invoke
- **Meaning:** To call on a rule, right, or process; to put something into use.
- **Usage:** Security can invoke the incident protocol if customer data is at risk.

### 9. offset
- **Meaning:** To balance or cancel out the effect of something else.
- **Usage:** The overtime cost was offset by finishing the project two weeks early.

### 10. quell
- **Meaning:** To stop or calm something, such as fear, doubt, or unrest.
- **Usage:** A clear status update helped quell concerns about the delayed launch.

## Phrases (10)

### 1. a drop in the ocean
- **Meaning:** A very small amount compared with what is needed.
- **Usage:** One extra hire is a drop in the ocean given the backlog we have.

### 2. bend over backwards
- **Meaning:** Try extremely hard to help or please someone.
- **Usage:** The support team bent over backwards to get the customer’s account restored.

### 3. come to terms with
- **Meaning:** Accept a difficult situation after struggling with it.
- **Usage:** It took a week to come to terms with the project being cancelled.

### 4. get the better of
- **Meaning:** Defeat someone; let emotion control you.
- **Usage:** Don’t let frustration get the better of you in the escalation call.

### 5. in the driver’s seat
- **Meaning:** In control of a situation.
- **Usage:** With the new budget approved, product is in the driver’s seat on priorities.

### 6. on thin ice
- **Meaning:** In a risky situation where more mistakes could cause serious trouble.
- **Usage:** After missing two deadlines, the vendor is on thin ice with us.

### 7. pull strings
- **Meaning:** Use influence or connections to get something done.
- **Usage:** She had to pull a few strings to get the conference room on short notice.

### 8. see the light at the end of the tunnel
- **Meaning:** Start to feel that a difficult period is almost over.
- **Usage:** After fixing the last blocker, we can finally see the light at the end of the tunnel.

### 9. turn a blind eye
- **Meaning:** Ignore something you know is wrong.
- **Usage:** We can’t turn a blind eye to repeated policy violations.

### 10. weather the storm
- **Meaning:** Survive a difficult period without giving up.
- **Usage:** The team weathered the storm of last quarter’s outages and came out stronger.
