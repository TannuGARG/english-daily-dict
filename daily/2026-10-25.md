# Daily English — 2026-10-25

Focus: intermediate vocabulary for daily personal life + working professionals.

## Words (10)

### 1. adapt
- **Meaning:** To change your approach so it fits a new situation.
- **Usage:** We need to adapt the demo script for a non-technical audience.

### 2. brief
- **Meaning:** To give someone a short, clear update or set of instructions.
- **Usage:** Can you brief me before the stakeholder call?

### 3. contradict
- **Meaning:** To say the opposite of something already said, or to go against it.
- **Usage:** The monitoring data seems to contradict yesterday’s incident report.

### 4. elaborate
- **Meaning:** To add more detail or explanation.
- **Usage:** Could you elaborate on why the timeline slipped by two days?

### 5. foster
- **Meaning:** To encourage something to grow or develop.
- **Usage:** Good standups foster open communication across teams.

### 6. highlight
- **Meaning:** To draw special attention to something important.
- **Usage:** Please highlight the risks in the first slide of the deck.

### 7. intervene
- **Meaning:** To get involved in a situation to change what is happening.
- **Usage:** If the vendor keeps missing deadlines, leadership may intervene.

### 8. prompt
- **Meaning:** Done without delay; also, to cause someone to take action.
- **Usage:** Thanks for the prompt reply — we can unblock the vendor now.

### 9. reinforce
- **Meaning:** To make an idea, habit, or structure stronger.
- **Usage:** Sharing weekly wins helps reinforce a culture of ownership.

### 10. validate
- **Meaning:** To check that something is correct, useful, or accepted.
- **Usage:** We should validate the API contract with the partner before coding.

## Phrases (10)

### 1. break the ice
- **Meaning:** Do or say something to make people feel more comfortable.
- **Usage:** A quick round of introductions helped break the ice in the workshop.

### 2. cut corners
- **Meaning:** Do something in a cheaper or faster way that reduces quality.
- **Usage:** We can’t cut corners on security just to ship this Friday.

### 3. get straight to the point
- **Meaning:** Speak directly without unnecessary details.
- **Usage:** In the email, get straight to the point about the budget ask.

### 4. have a lot on your plate
- **Meaning:** Be busy with many responsibilities.
- **Usage:** She has a lot on her plate this sprint, so let’s reassign the ticket.

### 5. keep an eye on
- **Meaning:** Watch something carefully for changes or problems.
- **Usage:** Keep an eye on the error rate after we deploy tonight.

### 6. make a judgment call
- **Meaning:** Decide based on experience when rules are unclear.
- **Usage:** You’ll have to make a judgment call if the client asks for a live change.

### 7. learn the ropes
- **Meaning:** Learn how a job or system works.
- **Usage:** It took a week to learn the ropes of our release process.

### 8. pick up where you left off
- **Meaning:** Continue from the point where you stopped earlier.
- **Usage:** Tomorrow we can pick up where we left off on the migration plan.

### 9. take something with a grain of salt
- **Meaning:** Not fully trust something; treat it cautiously.
- **Usage:** Take early vendor estimates with a grain of salt until we see a pilot.

### 10. wrap up
- **Meaning:** Finish or bring something to an end.
- **Usage:** Let’s wrap up the discussion and send action items by EOD.
